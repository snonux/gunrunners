The story

Neon City, in a 1987 that never ended. Six couriers, Dash, Rocco, Nova, Jade, Skye and the robot Bolt, get a call. A client called MAX, who only ever shows up as a scrambled hologram, has work for them:

MAX “Forty-two jobs. Paid by the job. Don't ask what's in the box.”

Every job is the same deal. Somewhere dangerous (a rooftop where the neon signs blink to the beat, a speeding maglev, a jungle temple, a frozen space station) a stolen prototype weapon sits in a green box. Get in, grab it, reach the exit, get paid, and don't ask questions.

The runners don't ask, but the clues pile up: a gunship that carries a camera lens, temple gold that weighs nothing, a laugh track in the middle of the jungle, and a space station AI that keeps apologising for “the script”.

Somebody is watching. And the ratings are through the roof.

The whole story, episode by episode (spoilers!)

The twist in plain words: it's all a TV show

What is going on?
The runners are the unknowing stars of a reality show called GUNRUNNERS on the TV network MaxTV. The jobs are the show: levels 1 to 21 are its first season, and Season Two follows. Some of the dangerous places are sets the network built: the temple's gold is painted fiberglass, Station Zero is a studio, and its AI, ZERO, has to read its lines from the show's script.
Who is watching?
A studio audience and viewers at home; the laughing in the jungle was the show's laugh track. Hidden cameras film the runners: the candid camera hidden in each of levels 1 to 20 is one of the show's, and so was the lens in the gunship. Even the bonus levels are the show's commercial breaks: “We'll be right back!”
Why the weapons?
The show's sponsor is the arms maker Halcyon Arms. The “stolen prototypes” are its new weapons, and every time a runner fires one on air, it is a live demonstration for arms buyers in forty countries. Without knowing it, the runners have been running guns for Halcyon: hence the show's name.
Who is MAX?
The client was Maxine Vale, the show's director, behind a scrambled hologram. She makes the show, but she is on the runners' side: she hid the cameras where they would spot them, to tip them off.
And Lance Marquee?
The show's host, the face of MaxTV, who only cares about the ratings.
How does it end?
Once the runners know, MaxTV just keeps filming them (Season Two). They break out through the static, Maxine leads them back to the studios, and in the live finale they switch off the network's broadcast and go on air themselves, to show the viewers what was really in the box.

Episode by episode

  1. Episode 1 · Neon Overdrive

    MAX sends the runners after one package through Neon City: up a rooftop, all the way up the Halcyon tower, into a night club, a blacked-out district, the sewers and onto the midnight maglev. By then they have “attracted attention”: the gunship Black Halo comes for them. MAX calls that excellent. In the wreck they find the case, stamped HALCYON ARMS, and a camera lens. Why would a gunship carry a camera?

  2. Episode 2 · Lost Temple

    Next, a jungle temple: “ancient, valuable, guarded”. The runners fight through traps, mines and lava up to the golden golem Kaan-Tolok. Its gold turns out to be painted fiberglass, the temple a tourist trap, and somewhere out there people are laughing.

  3. Episode 3 · Station Zero

    Then a research station in orbit, run by the AI ZERO. It welcomes them, apologises for the cold (“It's in the script”) and asks them to be quick when they reach it. Beaten, ZERO says thank you: it hated that script. A wall tips over and the station is a TV set. A studio audience cheers, and host Lance Marquee wraps “Season One of GUNRUNNERS”. The monitors replay the candid camera shots of the runners (one for every camera you found), and the lens from the gunship fits the studio cameras.

    The truth: the jobs were a reality show on MaxTV, and the prototypes they fetched were Halcyon Arms' weapons, demonstrated live to buyers in forty countries.

    DASH “We've been running their guns.”

    Before they can do anything about it, the floor opens and Season Two starts.

  4. Episode 4 · Channel Surfing

    The runners land inside the network, and every channel is a show starring them: a Western, a haunted manor, a cooking show, a nature film where they are the tiny wildlife, a kaiju movie where they are the monster, a film noir and finally a cartoon with its star, Buddy Boom. When the cartoon's iris closes, Nova jams it open and they all jump into the static. Lance panics: “Cut to commercial!”

  5. Episode 5 · Signal Hunt

    Lost in the dead air between channels, they hear a faint voice, S, telling them not to fight the noise but to tune it. They follow the signal under the sea, through a storm, across a dish array and a mainframe, up to the transmitter mast, where Halcyon Arms sends its biggest model, the Halcyon Titan, to stop them. At the top the dish swings round to the MaxTV studios: home. The voice gets a face: Maxine Vale, the show's director. She was MAX all along, and she is the one who hid the cameras where the runners would find them. They ride the signal beam back to the studio.

  6. Episode 6 · Live Finale

    Inside MaxTV, Maxine guides them through the prop warehouse, the wardrobe and the writers' room (“They're still writing your ending. Edit it.”) to the control gallery, where someone grabs her mid-sentence. The runners go after her up the fly tower and through a live audience on sweeps week, into the finale against Lance on his hover podium and his Encore Engine, and then a race to the master switch. ON AIR goes dark, and Maxine puts the runners out live on an independent broadcast.

    DASH “Forty-two jobs. Here's what was in the box.”

    They walk out into Neon City, where someone has painted RUN over the Halcyon logo. Clear every level from 1 to 42 with all its hidden caches and the letters G-U-N found, and a scene follows the credits.

  7. Episode 7 · Deep Space (a side story)

    Not part of the TV plot, playable any time. After a job the exit teleporter locks onto a stray signal, and the runners come out in an old courier ship over Vurr, a living alien planet. MAX on the radio: an alien relic, the Star Seed, is down there, and the Hive Mother wants it first. Seven levels later they fly it home.

Villains, bosses and other trouble

Almost every level brings its own residents. Some of the over 150 kinds of enemy, and the ones that run the show:

Black Halo

Level 7 · Chopper Down

A gunship with a searchlight and a rope full of troopers. Lock-On Rockets help. It also carries a camera lens, for some reason.

Kaan-Tolok, the Idol Golem

Level 14 · The Idol Awakens

The temple's golden guardian. Every gem you pick up feeds a greed meter that plates the golem in gold; gems offered at the altars bring it back down.

ZERO

Level 21 · ZERO

The station's AI rewires the level around you while you work out why it keeps saying “please be quick.”

Buddy Boom

Level 28 · Toon Town Throwdown

A rubber-hose cartoon star with a mallet, a pie and a giant pencil that draws new floors (and erases yours).

The Halcyon Titan

Level 35 · The Transmitter

Halcyon Arms' biggest demo model, sent up the transmitter mast to keep the signal from pointing home.

The Hive Mother

Level 49 · The Hive Mother

The queen of Vurr on a throne of eggs, with Spore Nurses to heal her. Shoot her crown when it glows. Then run.

MAX

The anonymous client: a scrambled hologram, a fat fee per job and a strict no-questions policy.

Halcyon Arms

Makers of the prototypes the runners keep fetching. Slogan: Peace through firepower.

Lance Marquee

All teeth and cue cards. Turns up later, and he has a hover podium.

Extras

Motor Pool & Deep Dive

Two levels built around the vehicles

Tank, helicopter, hoverbike, submarine, space ship and mech. Hits go to the armour, not your hearts.

Plays like Duke Nukem II, then some

Frame-accurate movement

The jump arc, ladders, hang bars and the flamethrower jetpack are ported from Duke Nukem II via RigelEngine.

Bonus levels

Find the TV static and press up: a short level with crazy rules, then back where you were. Beat it for a Bonus Star.

The Arsenal

Almost every level hides a prototype weapon. Reach the exit with it and it joins your collection, with your best kill count.

Secrets

A rubber duck in every level, candid cameras, hidden gem caches, the letters G-U-N and the number 42 somewhere.

A soundtrack per level

Synthwave, house, dub, marimba, slide guitar, a waltz, noir trumpet, big band, arena rock. All synthesized: no samples.

Play anywhere

Keyboard, most gamepads (Xbox, PlayStation, Switch Pro, 8BitDo, Steam Deck), or touch on Android. Five save slots plus quick save, optionally synced to an S3 bucket.

Get the game

Free software under the GNU GPL, version 2 or later. No ads and no accounts.

Android

Install with F-Droid from Paul's repository: open snonux/fdroid for the one-tap link and QR code, or add https://snonux.github.io/fdroid/repo under Settings > Repositories and search for Gunrunners.

On-screen thumb controls, or any Bluetooth or USB gamepad.

Linux

Grab gunrunners-vX.Y.Z-linux-x86_64.tar.gz from the latest release when it carries one, unpack it anywhere and start gunrunners. It needs SDL2, Cairo, libcurl and the DejaVu fonts. Or build it, on any architecture:

git clone https://github.com/snonux/gunrunners
cd gunrunners
sudo dnf install gcc-c++ cmake SDL2-devel cairo-devel libcurl-devel   # Fedora
sudo apt install build-essential cmake libsdl2-dev libcairo2-dev libcurl4-openssl-dev   # Debian/Ubuntu
./build.sh -r

The source

Everything is on GitHub: the C++ engine, all 49 levels as text files, the cutscene scripts, the synthesizer and the tools that drew this page's box art and recorded its clips.
